# OrderMate – Research

## 1. The problem (5 lines)

1. Hostel students use quick-commerce apps such as Blinkit, Zepto and Swiggy Instamart to buy daily essentials like milk, bread and eggs.
2. When a student needs only one or two items, delivery or other checkout charges can make the small order more expensive.
3. To avoid these charges or reach a platform's free-delivery condition, a student may add an item they did not originally need.
4. Students in the same or nearby hostels may be planning to order from the same platform, but they may not know about each other's plans.
5. They need a simple way to discover nearby, time-limited order invitations and ask to join, so they can coordinate purchases and possibly reduce avoidable costs.

## 2. Existing solutions and why they do not fit the intended user

### Solution 1: WhatsApp hostel groups

A student can ask in a WhatsApp group whether anyone nearby also wants to order. However, this does not fully fit the intended user:

- The intended user is a member of their own hostel's group, but not necessarily the groups of nearby hostels. They will not see an order request posted in another hostel's group unless they are added or receive an invite.
- WhatsApp does not provide one nearby feed of open shopping orders that can be sorted by platform, hostel, distance and time. A message can also get buried in normal group conversations.
- WhatsApp disappearing messages have fixed options of 24 hours, 7 days or 90 days. They do not provide a custom 50-minute expiry for one order invitation.

###  Solution 2: Ordering Individually by Paying Delivery Charges or Adding Extra Items

Students commonly use this approach when they need only a few daily essentials but do not meet the platform's free-delivery threshold.

- A student can either pay the applicable delivery charges or add more products to their cart to reach the required threshold.
- Adding unnecessary products increases their total spending, while paying delivery charges makes a small order more expensive.
- This approach does not help students discover other people in the same or nearby hostels who are planning to order from the same platform. They miss the opportunity to combine their requirements and potentially reduce their expenses.

## 3. Sources read (3 notes for each source)

### Source 1: WhatsApp Help Center – About disappearing messages

Link: https://faq.whatsapp.com/673193694148537

1. WhatsApp says disappearing messages can be set to disappear after 24 hours, 7 days or 90 days.
2. The setting applies to new messages in a chat; it is not a separate timer for each order post.
3. This confirms why a WhatsApp chat does not provide the custom 50-minute visibility period planned for an OrderMate order invitation.

### Source 2: Blinkit – Terms and Conditions

Link: https://blinkit.com/terms?mobile=1

1. Blinkit's terms list possible service charges, including delivery, handling, convenience, platform and small-cart charges.
2. The terms say service charges are shown at checkout before the order is placed.
3. The terms say charges may vary with factors such as cart value, distance, time of day and demand, so the actual charge must be checked for each order.

## 4. Proposed solution: OrderMate

OrderMate will help students discover and coordinate quick-commerce orders with people in the same or nearby hostels.

- A user posts the platform, the amount still needed to reach the relevant delivery condition, their hostel or approximate location, and how long the post should stay visible (for example, 50 minutes).
- Other users see active orders, with their own hostel and nearby hostels prioritised. They can send a join request and mention the amount they expect to spend.
- The order creator can accept or reject requests. After acceptance, participants can use a private chat to coordinate the purchase.
- Each user can view orders they created and orders they joined in their order history.
- After an order is completed, users enter their actual spending. Savings are calculated from the cost they would have paid ordering separately compared with their actual share of the combined order. The amount needed to reach a threshold is not counted as savings by itself.
